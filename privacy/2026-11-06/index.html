This policy explains how Immersia Labs LLC ("we", "us") handles information when you play Bubble Punks on iPhone, iPad or Android, use our game servers, or visit bubblepunks.com. We've tried to write it in plain words. If anything is unclear, email support@immersialabs.com.

The short version

  • You can play without telling us your real name, email address, phone number or where you live.
  • If you're 13 or over, when you set up the game we create a game account for you with a random ID and a player name, and we save your progress when you play online.
  • Solo games and Wi-Fi party games aren't sent to us: we never see what happens in them. When it opens while your phone is online, the game asks our servers for its settings, which sends your IP address but no account details. Once you have a game account, it also checks your sign-in with our servers each time it opens while your phone is online. Offline, it checks nothing, and solo and Wi-Fi games still work. If your account has a friends list, the game also stays connected while it's open, so your friends can see you're online.

  • Players under 13 play solo and Wi-Fi party games, with no game account. Apart from a few technical requests in the launch when the phone is set up for them, and one request to erase an account the phone already had, their phone sends us nothing about them or their games (section 5).
  • No ads. We don't sell your information, track you across other apps or websites, or use your information to train AI.
  • You can delete your account at any time in the app (Profile → Account → Delete account).

If you're under 13

Here's how Bubble Punks works for you:

  • You play solo games, and Wi-Fi party games with people in the same place.
  • You don't have an online account, and we don't know who you are.
  • We never ask for your real name, email, phone number, address or photo.
  • At a Wi-Fi party, you pick a party name made from words, like Bouncy Fox. Only the phones on the same Wi-Fi see it.
  • Your progress stays on your phone.
  • If you have questions, ask a parent or another grown-up you trust to help you.

2. What we collect

Playing solo or on Wi-Fi

  • Solo games aren't sent to us.
  • Wi-Fi party games don't reach us either. So that you can play together, your phone shares your player name, title, XP and level, character and the looks you wear directly with the other phones in the room. The host's phone announces the room to devices on the same Wi-Fi network, with its name (at first, your player name followed by "'s room") and a room code made from the phone's address on that network. Phones in the room connect to each other directly, so they learn each other's addresses on that network. Players under 13 pick their party name from word lists.
  • Once you have a game account (players 13 and over; see below), the game checks your sign-in with our servers each time it opens while your phone is online, even if you then play solo or on Wi-Fi. Offline, it checks nothing, and solo and Wi-Fi games still work. If your account has a friends list (accounts linked to Google or Apple), the game also stays connected to our servers while it's open, so your friends can see you're online and invite you. This sends the account details, IP address and sign-in records described below, but nothing about your solo or Wi-Fi games. Signing out (Profile → Account → Sign out) stops both.
  • Even without a game account, the game asks our servers for its settings (such as whether online play is open, and the current season) each time it opens while your phone is online. That request carries your IP address and the app version, but no account details. When the game downloads new game files, such as posters, looks or maps, it fetches them from Google Cloud's file storage the same way: the file's name, your IP address and the app version, nothing else. On a phone set up for a player under 13, these requests stop too (section 5).
  • Your settings, such as controls, sound, your player name, your age group and the month it changes, are stored on your phone (see "On your phone" in section 8).

Crash reports

If the game crashes or hits a serious error, it sends a crash report to Firebase Crashlytics, Google's crash-reporting service. A report holds where in the game's code the problem happened, the app version, your phone's model and operating system, how much memory and storage were free at that moment, and the time. It also carries random IDs for this installation of the game (from Firebase and from Crashlytics), so that we can tell how many phones a problem affects. For errors in the game's own code, a report carries the type of error and where it happened, never the error's own message. We don't put your account ID, player name or anything you typed into crash reports, and we don't link them to your account. When the game opens, Firebase also registers the installation ID with Google and may send a short note that the game started, even if nothing crashes, so that crash-free sessions can be counted. We use all of this only to find and fix problems. On a phone set up for a player under 13, crash reports stop (section 5). Section 8 says how long they're kept.

Players 13 and over can type a player name and room names, which other players can see. Nobody types a title: titles come from the game.

3. How we use information

We use information only to:

  • Run the game: sign you in, save your progress, run rooms and matches (including matching players of similar skill), and let you play with friends.
  • Keep the game safe and fair: stop cheating, abuse, spam and attacks, enforce our rules, and protect players, especially children.
  • Keep it working: run our servers, and find and fix problems, such as lag or crashes.
  • Follow the law: meet our legal duties, enforce our terms and respond to lawful requests.
  • Talk with you: answer your messages.

We don't:

  • show ads, or use your information for marketing or to build a picture of your interests;
  • sell or rent your information, or share it for advertising;
  • track you across other apps or websites;
  • use your information to train AI, or let the company behind our AI tools train on it. When AI tools help us analyze or debug the game, they get only what section 6 says;
  • make automated decisions about you that have legal or similarly significant effects;
  • send push notifications. If we add them, we'll ask your permission first, and we won't send them to players under 13.

5. Children under 13

This section is our notice to parents under the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA).

How we find out a player's age

When the game is first set up, the app asks for the player's birth month and year, with nothing pre-selected. The app works out the age group on the phone. For a player 13 or over, it sends only the age group to our servers, and for players 13 to 17 the month they move up to the next group. For a player under 13, it sends nothing about the child: the phone only remembers that it's set up for a player under 13, and the game, as installed, doesn't ask again. The birthday itself is never stored. Until the question is answered, the game creates no account.

Where Apple or Google shares an age range. Some state laws require app stores to check their users' ages and share an age range with apps. Where your store shares one, the game uses it as well as asking, and checks it again each time the game opens. If the store's range is younger than the age given in the game, the store's applies. If it says the player is under 13, the game shows only solo and Wi-Fi play on that phone while it says so. If the game account on that phone was made in this install of the game, the game asks us to erase the account (below). If the account was made on another phone, or it's a sign-in Apple kept from an earlier install (section 8, "On your phone"), it may be a parent's, so it stays on our servers, and the store's answer isn't sent to us. But while the store says so, the game uses the account for nothing, and crash reports stop and their installation ID is deleted, as on a phone set up for a player under 13. If the store still needs to check the player's age, the game shows only solo and Wi-Fi play until it has. For players 13 and over, we keep only the age group the store's range maps to (and, for some players, the store approval ID in section 2), never the range itself.

What a phone set up for a player under 13 does

  • Solo and Wi-Fi party games only. There's no game account, no online play, no friends list, no purchases and no push notifications. Progress stays on the phone.
  • At a Wi-Fi party, the child picks a party name from our word lists. It goes only to the other phones on the same Wi-Fi network, with the child's look and level, as section 2 describes. It never reaches us.
  • The game sends nothing to us or to Google about the child or their games, apart from asking Apple or Google for the age range they already hold.
  • The exceptions. In the launch in which the phone is set up for a player under 13 (usually the first time the game opens, or the launch in which Apple, Google or our servers tell the game), until the game closes, a few things already under way can still go out: crash reports (section 2), Firebase's registration of the installation and its note that the game started, and the game's requests for its settings, the current season, its content list and game files. They carry only the phone's IP address and the app version, and, for crash reports and Firebase, a random installation ID and details of the phone; never the age group or anything about the child. The game then deletes that installation ID from Firebase; if the phone is offline then, it does so at the next launch that can reach Firebase.
  • An account the phone already had. If the game was already signed in to a game account on the phone before it was set up for a player under 13, the game also sends one request to erase it: the request signs in as that account and tells our server it's a child's, so we erase it. If it can't go through (for example, while the phone is offline), the game tries again at later launches until it has. Apart from these two (the erase request, and deleting the installation ID) until each has gone through, nothing goes out from the next launch. A sign-in that Apple kept from an earlier install of the game (section 8, "On your phone") doesn't count: it may be a parent's, so when a new install is set up for a player under 13, the game forgets that sign-in and sends nothing about it.
  • Children can't make personal information public. Their Wi-Fi party name comes from our word lists, and nothing they do reaches anyone outside the Wi-Fi party.

What we collect from children, and why

From a child's phone, we may collect only what the exceptions above describe: the phone's IP address and, in crash reports and Firebase's registration, random IDs for this installation of the game (from Firebase and Crashlytics), with technical details about the phone (its model, operating system and free memory, the app version, and where the game's code failed). If the phone already had a game account, the erase request also tells us that account is a child's, and we then erase it. We collect no other personal information from children: no name, email address, phone number, photos, voice, location, player name or game history.

We don't ask a parent's permission first, because we use these identifiers only to support the internal operations of the game, which are:

  1. sending the game its settings and files over the internet (network communications);
  2. keeping the game working, and finding and fixing crashes;
  3. protecting our servers against abuse and attacks;
  4. erasing a game account the phone already had, and meeting our other legal duties.

How we make sure the information is used for nothing else:

  • The app contains no advertising, analytics or ad-tracking tools, and we show no ads.
  • We never use it to contact a child, or to build a picture of a child's interests or behavior, and we never sell it.
  • The settings, content and file requests, and crash reports, carry no age group, account ID, player name or anything typed. In our own systems, IP addresses appear only in our server logs, in a scrambled form, and for a few minutes in our servers' memory to stop abuse. They're never joined to anything else, and the logs are deleted within 30 days (section 2). Google also receives the IP address with each request it handles for us (section 6). Crash reports carry no age group, name or anything typed, and Google deletes them after 90 days; the game deletes the installation ID from Firebase when the phone is set up for a player under 13.
  • Only the people who run Bubble Punks can look at it, and only for the uses above. AI tools never get a child's information. If one ever did by mistake, we'd treat it as a security incident. Our written security program sets these limits, and everyone with access works under it.

Who we share children's information with

  • Service providers that run parts of the game for us: Google (our servers and game files on Google Cloud, crash reports in Firebase Crashlytics, and our email, if a parent or child writes to us). They may use it only to provide those services to us.
  • Google's own services: Google also keeps some records of requests to its services under its own terms (section 6). If a parent or child calls our support line, the call goes through Google Voice, Google's phone service, under Google's own terms.
  • Our website's host, GitHub, records the IP address of everyone who visits bubblepunks.com, children included, to keep its service secure (section 13).
  • Legal reasons and safety: when the law requires it, or to protect someone's safety, or to stop fraud, abuse or security problems (section 6).
  • If our company changes hands: the new owner must keep the promises in this section, and must get a parent's permission before using a child's information in any new way (section 6).

We don't share children's information with anyone else, apart from those listed in this section, and we never sell it or share it for advertising.

Parents' rights

A child's phone has no game account, so usually we hold nothing linked to your child that we could show you: the scrambled IP addresses in our logs and the crash reports can't be tied to a child.

If your child had a game account, we erase it when we learn it's a child's (below). If you'd like a copy of its information first, say so when you write, and we'll send it before we erase it, once we've checked the request. An erasure that starts from the phone happens on its own, within a day. After erasure we keep only our record that the account was erased (12 months), our record of your request if you wrote to us (24 months), purchase records without the account, as tax and accounting law requires, and backups until they're replaced (up to 30 days).

You can ask us questions, or ask us to delete anything you think we hold about your child. To stop any further collection, delete the game from the phone.

To make a request, email support@immersialabs.com with the subject "Parent request", write to us at 30 N Gould St Ste N, Sheridan, WY 82801, or call +1 510-936-1426. We'll reply within 30 days.

We never ask a child for more information than they need. If a child under 13 emails us directly, we reply once and then delete their email address and message. If a child calls us or leaves a voicemail, we don't call back, and we delete the voicemail and the call from our phone as soon as we've dealt with it (Google Voice keeps its own call records under Google's terms). A letter from a child gets no reply by post. If a child's message tells us someone is in danger, we keep it only to pass it to the police or child-protection services, or to answer their request.

If we learn a child has a game account

If we learn that a player under 13 has a game account, we erase it. When the game or the store tells us (the game was set up for a player under 13 while signed in to the account, or Apple or Google says the player is under 13 in the install of the game where the account was made), that happens within a day. When a parent tells us, we first check that the request comes from the account's holder, as section 9's deletion does; meanwhile we stop using the account, and once we've checked, we erase it promptly, within 10 days. If it had bought anything, we keep those purchase records, without the account, only as tax and accounting law requires. The child's phone then shows only solo and Wi-Fi play.

It includes reports made about you, with their reason, date and outcome, but never who made them, and a list of the actions our staff took on your account, and when.
  • Correct it. Change your player name on the Profile screen.
  • Delete it. In the app: Profile → Account → Delete account. Without the app: see bubblepunks.com/delete-account. Section 8 says what's erased, and when.
  • Sign out. If your account is linked to Google or Apple, sign out in Profile → Account, on this phone or on all your devices. Update the game before you rely on signing out on a shared phone. Deleting it starts the game over on that phone as a new player.

Removing something doesn't guarantee it's completely gone everywhere: for example, someone may have taken a screenshot, and copies stay in our logs and backups until they're deleted (section 8). A closed room keeps its name in our records until your account is erased, and a name someone reported stays in that report until the report is deleted.

  • Use extra rights in some states. Some US state laws, such as Connecticut's, also give you the right to a copy of your information in a format you can reuse, and to opt out of targeted advertising, the sale of your information, and profiling used for decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. We don't do any of those things (our skill rating only picks fair opponents), and we send copies in a common format such as JSON. We'll answer your appeal within 60 days, and if we still say no, we'll tell you how to complain to your state's attorney general.

10. Security

  • Sign-in, account and room traffic is encrypted on its way between your phone and our servers (HTTPS and secure WebSockets).
  • During a match, your moves travel in signed packets: our servers throw away any packet that has been changed on the way. They aren't encrypted, because they contain only game moves.
  • Our server disks and backups are encrypted. Our servers keep sign-in tokens, recovery codes and room passwords only in scrambled (hashed) form (except the Apple sign-in token, which we need whole to cancel your Apple sign-in, and which is useless without our own Apple key), and scramble IP addresses when they write them into logs on purpose (see "About IP addresses" in section 2).
  • Only the people who run Bubble Punks can access player data, and we follow a written security program that we review every year. AI tools get player data only as section 6 says, and every such use is recorded.
  • Our admin console shows each person only what their job needs, one account at a time, needs two-step sign-in with a passkey, and records every look at a player's account and every change. Our server commands need two-step sign-in too, and record every run.

No system is perfectly secure. If a security breach affects your information, we'll tell the people affected and the authorities as the law requires. Because we don't have most players' email addresses, we may tell you in the game or on our website.
